# Review of the AAISM study guide

Reviewed 20 October 2026. Repository: `jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide`, commit `10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5` (10 August 2026). Scope: README, all 31 chapters, four front-matter sections, both end-matter sections, all 25 self-test questions, and the text and structure of the 24 diagrams. This is the historical review of v2.x. The subsequent v3.0 implementation is recorded in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md); line links below refer to the reviewed commit.

The benchmark is the public [ISACA exam content outline](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/aaism/aaism-exam-content-outline), supplemented by primary technical and regulatory sources linked beside findings. I did not compare the guide with the paid official review manual or confidential exam questions. Comments about depth, teaching quality and readiness are editorial judgments, not ISACA endorsements or predictions of a passing score. Browser rendering, mobile layout and printed output were not tested.

The guide has a useful teaching structure: explanations usually connect a risk to its mechanism and then to a control. Retrieval permissions, agent authority, evidence retention and the distinction between securing AI and using AI for security are particularly effective. The chief weakness is precision. Memorable simplifications repeatedly become universal rules, and the practice questions reinforce some of those rules. I would revise the issues below before presenting it as sufficient preparation on its own.

## Front matter and README

**README — revise the promise and the metadata.** The inventory of features and offline instructions is helpful. Correct the certification name at line 10. “Everything you need” and “complete syllabus coverage” exceed what the current self-test and unmapped coverage demonstrate. Describe it as an independent study companion until the coverage and assessment have been strengthened. The README promises a fictional company throughout every chapter, but explicit Calder references occur in only chapters 4, 5, 7 and 8. Either extend that teaching device through the domain chapters or narrow the claim. Prefer the repository's GitHub Pages address over the third-party HTML preview link as the main reading URL. The header says v2.2 while the HTML footer at line 3656 says v2.1; align them.

**About this guide — retain, add traceability.** Source: [index.html:347](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L347). The independence statement and acknowledgements establish useful boundaries. Add the specific outline version or verification date, source URLs and a correction history. The coverage statement needs a chapter-and-question mapping to the 22 supporting tasks. Distinguish the domain percentages from study-time allocation: matching three labels does not prove proportional instructional coverage. “The dependency map on the previous page” is also incorrect in the present ordering; the map comes later.

**How to read this book — repair stale instructions.** Source: [index.html:388](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L388). Asking readers to retrieve explanations from memory is good teaching. However, Part II has six chapters, not three, and “four kinds of box” introduces five examples. Later chapters use still more box types without a stable explanation of their purpose. The detailed opener/objectives/takeaways format appears in chapters 3–8 but is not maintained through the domain material. Adopt a smaller consistent set of devices and give readers a route based on a brief diagnostic rather than assuming every CISM/CISSP holder has the same gaps. Add model answers or a scoring rubric for the chapter recall questions.

**The whole syllabus, mapped — useful overview; needs evidence underneath.** Source: [index.html:406](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L406), Figure 1. All 13 public subtopic branches are represented, which makes this a good revision map. Several embedded slogans inherit the guide's factual issues, including incompatible fairness definitions, irreversible deletion and rigid control-stage matching. Add chapter links and question references under each branch. Label details added by the author separately from the official outline so readers can distinguish the syllabus from an interpretation of it.

**How the ideas connect — revise the actual dependencies.** Source: [index.html:562](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L562), Figure 2. The visual organization and Calder introduction give the book a useful through-line. Several chapter numbers are stale: trust boundaries are in chapter 20, the attack taxonomy in 17, agents in 21, incidents in 14, and data/ethics in 23–24. The prose also promises an incident in chapter 13, which currently discusses enterprise risk. Remove the visible production note about diagrams “being aligned” to the palette. The case study would become substantive if later chapters produced Calder's charter, inventory, risk record, release decision and incident response using the same systems introduced here.

## Chapters 1–8: orientation and foundations

**1. What the exam actually tests — retain, qualify the authority.** Source: [index.html:662](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L662), `#c1`. The explanation that a scaled score is not a percentage is helpful. The domain breakdown gives readers a sensible starting point. Assertions about long stems, what the exam will never ask, and the subtopic candidates most often neglect need evidence or softer wording. The public outline does not establish those claims. Figure 3 appropriately marks question counts as approximate; also mark the “unfamiliarity” shading as an author estimate and reserve time for review rather than allocating the entire sitting to first-pass answers. Recommend study time from a diagnostic, with the domain weights as a baseline. Add dated links for eligibility and logistics.

**2. Thinking like the examiner — major revision.** Source: [index.html:720](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L720), `#c2`. Teaching readers to attend to FIRST/BEST/MOST and the stated role is useful. The problem is the claimed near-universality of assessment, governance and business-owner choice. Security can have delegated authority to halt activity; legal and safety requirements constrain every risk owner; avoiding a particular AI use is legitimate and is explicitly taught later. Add three counterexamples: active exfiltration requiring immediate restriction, a prohibited use that cannot be approved, and a scenario where governance is complete and technical control implementation is next. Figure 4 must change with the prose. Present these as reasoning aids, not privileged knowledge of ISACA's marking logic.

**3. The office — retain as an analogy, tighten its limits.** Source: [index.html:774](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L774), `#c31`. The handbook-versus-lock and badge-versus-authority examples explain why prompt instructions cannot replace access control. However, saying every security property has an “exact” office equivalent oversells the analogy. The model can receive role and source distinctions; those distinctions do not guarantee compliance. Instruction hierarchy research demonstrates that prioritization can improve, so “everything looks identical” is too literal. A vector store may contain source text, chunks and metadata alongside embeddings, rather than merely pointers. Label the analogy as one way of understanding LLM applications and add a short note on classifiers, recommenders and non-agent systems. See [instruction hierarchy research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208).

**4. The map of the field — correct the taxonomy and risk shortcuts.** Source: [index.html:892](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L892), `#c28`. The supervised/unsupervised/reinforcement introduction is concise and useful. “Predictive,” “generative” and “agentic” are overlapping capabilities and deployment patterns, not three exclusive model families. Generative methods are not universally a subset of deep learning, although modern foundation-model examples usually are. Overfitting is poor generalization and can increase privacy exposure; it is not identical to memorizing every training record. Underfitting can also be subtle and harmful, so remove the claim that it is the safer, visible failure. Define ANI/AGI/ASI as background terminology with contested boundaries, not a universal regulatory classification. Show an example of a generative model inside an agent to make the overlap concrete.

**5. Where AI belongs, and where it does not — retain the decision, relax the absolutes.** Source: [index.html:1026](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1026), `#c30`. The invoice comparison makes the case for considering a simpler system before buying AI. The decision table becomes too categorical: stable high-impact decisions can sometimes use an interpretable statistical model, and an unfamiliar workflow does not automatically require an agent. AI does not necessarily adapt after deployment. Claims about the dominant project failure cause need an original study or should be described as common patterns. Replace “AI output must never enter training without human review” with risk-based validation, provenance and quality controls; validated synthetic training data is a legitimate technique. Extend the invoice example with false approvals, false rejections, exception volume and total operating cost so the decision rests on measurable consequences.

**6. What a model is, and why it breaks your assumptions — highest-priority technical rewrite.** Source: [index.html:1106](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1106), `#c3`. The important point is sound: deleting a source record does not by itself remove its influence from a trained model. The chapter goes much further and claims no future engineering can change this. Machine unlearning research makes that claim untenable. Explain full retraining, specialized unlearning, evaluation of residual influence and the limits of output suppression; do not imply a compensating control automatically satisfies an erasure duty. [Bourtoule et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817) provide a concrete example, and the [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/our-work-on-artificial-intelligence/generative-ai-fourth-call-for-evidence/) treats the practical rights problem as an active engineering question.

Also separate probabilistic predictions from stochastic sampling. Many models produce repeatable outputs with fixed inputs and configuration. Statistical evaluations can still yield pass/fail release gates, and application invariants can be tested deterministically. “We tested it and it worked” can be weak evidence without being evidence of nothing. RAG and fine-tuning solve different problems and can be combined. RAG still exposes retrieved data to inference services, logs, caches and connectors; deleting the source does not instantly purge all copies. Figures 7 and 8 need the same corrections. Keep the useful emphasis on embeddings inheriting source sensitivity, while explaining that a vector store can retain the actual text too.

**7. How AI systems get built — good lifecycle explanation; make its gates consistent.** Source: [index.html:1279](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1279), `#c4`. The maintenance-model scenario, named acceptance criteria and distinction between data and concept drift work well. The claim that “nothing passes or fails” contradicts the evaluation gate the chapter then recommends. Make the gate explicit: specified metrics, representative data, uncertainty, minimum subgroup performance and approval authority. Reconcile this seven-stage account with chapter 22's seven phases: the first starts with sourcing, while the second includes planning and combines acquisition/preparation. State that these are two views, not competing canonical lists. Dataset versions, seeds and configuration support traceability without always guaranteeing bit-for-bit reproducibility. Treat the Zillow example as an illustration of forecasting and business-model risk; its public shareholder account does not prove one isolated drift cause or absence of internal monitoring. [Zillow's original account](https://s24.q4cdn.com/723050407/files/doc_financials/2021/q3/Zillow-Group-Q3%2721-Shareholder-Letter.pdf) is a suitable citation.

**8. Four ways to consume AI — replace the one-dimensional taxonomy.** Source: [index.html:1351](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1351), `#c5`. The unnoticed SaaS feature is a good procurement and discovery scenario. Fine-tuning, hosting and building are separate axes: a fine-tuned model can be vendor-hosted or self-hosted, and a self-hosted model can be an unmodified third-party model. The current categories make readers infer control and legal role from a label that does not determine either. Use a matrix of hosting, model ownership/adaptation, data processing and operational control. Qualify the assertion that egress filtering is beside the point when self-hosting; tool calls, telemetry and exfiltration can still cross the network. Keep the three discovery channels, but avoid assuming every developer download or widespread policy breach has the same motivation. Add an example comparing a privately hosted base model with a vendor-hosted fine-tune.

## Chapters 9–14: governance

**9. Stakeholders and the governance structure — retain, turn lists into decisions.** Source: [index.html:1437](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1437), `#c6`. The stakeholder table, review loop and reassessment triggers are among the stronger governance material. Distinguish an enterprise governance charter from an individual project charter; the chapter uses both meanings. Readiness assessment and governance can develop iteratively, so the heading “readiness comes before governance” is too rigid. Add a compact RACI showing who proposes, assesses, approves, operates, challenges and assures one high-risk use. Include delegated emergency authority and escalation thresholds, which are needed to correct chapter 2. Fix the references to inventory in chapter 8 and policy in chapter 7; those subjects are now chapters 11 and 10.

**10. Appetite, strategy and policy — consolidate and operationalize.** Source: [index.html:1556](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1556), `#c7`. Quantitative thresholds and the full cost of ownership are useful. The two business-case sections overlap and can be merged. Strategy and appetite inform each other; they are not always a strict one-way sequence. Distinguish appetite, tolerance and control thresholds explicitly. Add one worked example with an owner, a stated limit, evidence, an exception expiry and an escalation route. The closing policy-breach example should investigate user needs while containing an actual confidential-data exposure under existing authority. Policy enforcement and addressing causes can occur together. A measurable policy-compliance rate is also not, by itself, evidence that harm is acceptably low.

**11. Assets and the data estate — good list; needs an inventory example.** Source: [index.html:1625](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1625), `#c8`. The expanded asset definition and permitted-use dimension are practical. A reader should leave with one populated entry and a dependency chain connecting a service to its model, dataset, vector store, prompts and credentials. “At least annually” is an insufficient operational refresh target for changing AI assets; use event-driven updates with periodic reconciliation. Encourage safe disclosure without promising absolute immunity from misconduct consequences. Distinguish who coordinates discovery from who is accountable for each entry. Apply the chapter 6 correction wherever deletion is described as irreversibly impossible.

**12. Running the program — retain; distinguish coverage from effectiveness.** Source: [index.html:1654](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1654), `#c9`. Integrating with existing security processes and teaching role-specific AI literacy are useful recommendations. Several “control effectiveness” measures are completion or coverage measures: an impact assessment completed before deployment does not establish that its recommendations worked. Add an effectiveness measure, such as adversarial-test failure rate, substantiated harmful outcomes or tested override effectiveness, alongside coverage. Define denominators and acknowledge undiscovered assets when reporting inventory completeness. Replace “auditability reduces to three questions” with a fuller evidence chain including purpose, version, approval, control operation and outcomes. Add a short program roadmap with owners, resources, dependencies and review dates.

**13. AI risk in the enterprise picture — retain; qualify governance structure.** Source: [index.html:1698](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1698), `#c22`. Shared-provider concentration and aggregation are well explained. A specialist AI register can be useful if it feeds the enterprise process; separate storage is not inherently failed governance. Similarly, security can operate first-line controls while other teams provide challenge; a function's name does not permanently assign it to a line. Tailor independent validation to risk and applicable sector requirements rather than requiring a second-line model-validation function for every model. Add one risk statement that connects several apparently low-risk uses to a shared-provider outage and an enterprise tolerance.

**14. Incidents and continuity — strong coverage, inconsistent priorities.** Source: [index.html:1778](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1778), `#c10`. AI-specific responders, multiple reporting clocks, kill switches and the “available but untrustworthy” failure are useful additions to conventional response. The chapter nevertheless says notification always follows containment and places notification in the post-incident row. Make notification a parallel, deadline-driven workstream, and prioritize preventing imminent harm under the response plan. The later recognition of several clocks should change the earlier absolute rule. Preserve relevant evidence during containment rather than assuming it will remain available. The prompt-injection response table leans on filtering and fixed templates after chapter 17 correctly explains why they are insufficient; add revoked tool access, blocked exfiltration paths, isolation of tainted content and revalidation of the attack path. Expand continuity with a worked BIA, RTO/RPO, manual-fallback capacity, dependency restoration and a tested transition back to AI. Require an approved safe fallback, which may be manual, deterministic or independently validated, instead of asserting a non-AI replacement is universally possible. [NIST SP 800-61r3](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf) supports coordinated reporting during response.

## Chapters 15–19: risk

**15. Why AI risk behaves oddly — retain the broader harms; deepen risk analysis.** Source: [index.html:1866](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1866), `#c11`. The categories and concrete loss-event framing are useful, as is the explicit option to avoid a use case. The organization-versus-people distinction overstates the separation between risk assessment and impact assessment: a mature risk process can include stakeholder harm. An impact assessment makes those impacts explicit and systematic rather than inventing them. Add inherent, residual and target risk; likelihood and impact assumptions; uncertainty; treatment owner; acceptance authority; review date; and escalation when the residual risk exceeds tolerance. Replace acceptance based only on treatment cost with acceptance constrained by law, appetite and delegated authority. Use a complete Calder risk-register entry and show the effect of one control on that entry.

**16. Four assessments, and the language of money — separate the assessments from the quantification lesson.** Source: [index.html:1916](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L1916), `#c29`. The provider/conformity versus deployer/FRIA distinction is valuable. Add an applicability table so readers know when each assessment is required, by whom and with which output. A common intake should support the applicable assessments; it should not imply all four statutory outputs are mandatory for every AI use. Name DPIA when discussing that legal instrument and distinguish it from a general PIA. The FRIA trigger needs the actual eligible deployers and uses, including the relevant private credit/insurance uses and exceptions, rather than mainly “public bodies.” The Commission's [Article 27 text](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-27) supports that more precise scope.

The five generic quantification steps do not teach what makes FAIR distinctive. Introduce loss-event frequency and loss magnitude, uncertainty ranges, and primary versus secondary losses, then work a small example. For illustration, a mean 0.2 events/year and mean loss of $500,000/event gives a rough $100,000 annual expected loss, under the stated simplifying assumptions. Show why the distribution and severe tail still matter and why a cheaper treatment is not automatically permissible. The [FAIR Institute's own explanation](https://www.fairinstitute.org/blog/fair-risk-basics-what-is-loss-magnitude) supplies the core factors; [The Open Group's competency outline](https://help.opengroup.org/hc/en-us/articles/32130434422290-Competency-to-Role-Mapping-Open-FAIR-2-Foundation) also identifies them. Correct “governing body — the owners”: a governing body is not simply synonymous with the owners, and operational limits may be delegated.

**17. The threat landscape — useful breadth; rebuild the control mapping.** Source: [index.html:2006](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L2006), `#c12`. The attack definitions and distinction between OWASP's weakness list and ATLAS's adversary knowledge are useful. The main teaching rule is false as an absolute: an inference-time attack can have a training-time defence, and a compromised artifact can be introduced after training. Supply-chain and serialization attacks also span acquisition, deployment and runtime. Replace the binary control rule with a table containing attack objective, attacker access, attack stage, preventive controls, runtime detection and recovery. Differential privacy is not a general defence against copying model functionality; model extraction is not necessarily defeated by query limits. Avoid presenting any listed technique as a guarantee. Replace “there is no patch cycle” with a distinction between model-behaviour remediation and patching the application, dependencies and infrastructure. Pin OWASP and ATLAS versions/dates. Keep the emphasis on architectural constraints against injection, consistent with [OWASP's layered mitigations](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/).

**18. When AI is the attacker's tool — one of the stronger chapters; qualify its claims.** Source: [index.html:2192](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L2192), `#c23`. Pre-established callbacks, dual authorization and transaction limits give readers practical actions. Teach a callback as an independent, trusted verification path, with account compromise and SIM-swap limits acknowledged, rather than a guarantee attached to any phone call. Claims that awareness is obsolete or every effort-based control stops working are too broad; the pressure-pattern training recommended later in the same chapter is valuable awareness. Likewise, the Arup case does not establish there was nothing observable to any SOC or fraud system: transaction and account behaviour can still provide signals. Label Figure 14 as a qualitative illustration rather than measured cost data. Add one exercise redesigning payment authorization after a deepfake attempt.

**19. Vendors and the supply chain — substantial material; fix role and tier errors.** Source: [index.html:2254](https://github.com/jayelbotvibe-web/isaca-aaism-study-guide/blob/10d1474e4daf36ed0b5f10655764d3f6d7d27ac5/index.html#L2254), `#c13`. The model/data/assurance questions, scoped certifications, ongoing evaluation and exit provisions are practical. The shared-responsibility table needs separate columns for model development, hosting and adaptation: self-hosting an unchanged model does not mean the organization developed the base model or automatically acquired every provider obligation. The regulatory-role row should say “determine from the facts and applicable law,” followed by examples. Article 25 has specific [high-risk role-change conditions](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-25); GPAI models require their own analysis.

The supplier-tier paragraph at line 2303 is shifted: your direct external supplier is ordinarily your third party; its subcontractor is your fourth party. The guide calls the provider's directly contracted suppliers your third parties. Show a named chain instead of making readers infer it. ## Suggested replacement wording

For the central data-deletion explanation:

> Removing a record from the source dataset does not by itself remove its influence from an existing trained model. Depending on the model and the required assurance, remediation may involve retraining, a suitable unlearning method, or changes to the system and its use. Each approach needs evaluation. Output filtering alone does not prove that information has been removed or that a legal erasure obligation has been satisfied.

For the exam reasoning rules:

> Choose the next action from the scenario's facts, the role's authority, the current stage of work and applicable obligations. Establish missing context before choosing a treatment when there is no immediate harmful exposure. Where harm is active, use the authorized response plan to contain it while coordinating required escalation and notifications. A risk owner can accept only risks within their authority and the law.

For the privacy paragraph:

> Data collected for one purpose is not automatically available for model training. Establish an appropriate lawful basis and assess the new purpose under the applicable law before processing. If relying on consent, ensure that it validly covers the intended processing; other legal bases or additional conditions may apply depending on the data, purpose and jurisdiction.

## Recommended revision sequence

1. Correct the credential name, deletion and evaluation claims, legal-role/consent errors, and unconditional response rules. Apply each correction to prose, diagrams, glossary and answer explanations together.
2. Repair references, version metadata and the source bibliography. Add dates to changeable legal and standards material.
3. Fix the quiz reveal behavior and answer-position skew; revise the questions identified above before expanding the bank.
4. Carry Calder through the domain chapters with concrete artifacts: charter, inventory, risk entry, supplier contract schedule, architecture, release evidence, monitoring rule and tested fallback.
5. Add the 22-task traceability map, unseen practice scenarios and a study plan that adapts to measured gaps.

The explanation-first structure is worth preserving. The revision should make its good intuitions reliable under exceptions and give readers enough evidence and practice to apply them.
